Arbiter: signal flow and wiring, permission method

One radio, several programs (Subspace, fldigi, VarAC with VARA) through flrig. This is the collision-avoidance design of the test set: a program asks before it transmits. The earlier design, collision detection, is in arbiter_diagrams_CD. Arbiter 1.0.8-study18 (test set, not released), WM8Q, 2026-11-23.

No program transmits until the Arbiter says yes. One program has the radio at a time, and whatever has the radio finishes. The Arbiter never keys the radio and never refuses, swallows or delays a PTT: a program that waits has not asked flrig for PTT yet.

1. Signal flow: who asks, who answers, who sees what

normal CAT traffic (as before) the Arbiter sees (read only) a program asks the Arbiter answers VarAC's modem connection, through the Arbiter

4. Wiring: what is actually connected to what

Cables and port numbers. Only three things reach the radio: one audio path, one CAT line, one antenna. The Arbiter touches none of them, but it now stands in one connection inside the PC: the one between VarAC and VARA.

5. Reading the wiring

  • Only three things reach the radio: the audio (one sound device, shared by every program through the operating system's mixer), the serial/USB CAT line (owned by flrig alone), and the antenna. The Arbiter has none of them.
  • flrig is the only program on the serial line. Every program keys and tunes by asking flrig on 12345. With CAT Watch in front, flrig moves to 12346 and CAT Watch answers 12345; no program notices.
  • The Arbiter has five wires, all inside the PC: 12345 to flrig (reads PTT and the dial, never keys), 2442 to Subspace, 7362 to fldigi, VarAC's modem connection, and its own connection on to VARA.
  • VarAC and VARA are no longer wired to each other. VarAC keeps its usual ports (8300, 8301, KISS 8100) and finds the Arbiter there. VARA is moved to 8310, 8311 and KISS 8110, where the Arbiter finds VARA. Everything passes unchanged, except while another program has the radio.
  • This is the one place where the Arbiter is in a program's path. Subspace and fldigi are untouched when the Arbiter stops; VarAC then has no VARA until the Arbiter runs again.
  • Audio is where collisions become real: two programs keying at once put two signals into one audio channel on one keyed radio. Asking first prevents that; the audio wiring is never changed.

6. Limits worth knowing

  • A program that does not ask (a released Subspace or fldigi, any other program on flrig) is not avoided, only detected, as in the earlier design: one look (200 ms) of reaction time.
  • Keying that bypasses flrig (VOX, a separate PTT line, the radio's own buttons) is invisible to the Arbiter. A keyed radio nobody owns stops every grant until it un-keys.
  • Asking ends by itself when the Arbiter stops answering (Subspace 15 s, fldigi 3 s): both then transmit as without the Arbiter. VarAC cannot: its modem connection goes through the Arbiter.
  • After a message that expects a reply, Subspace keeps the radio until the reply is in or its time runs out. The other programs wait that long.
  • After a VarAC CQ, beacon or broadcast the radio is free at once. A caller who answers a CQ can find VarAC held, if another program was granted in that moment.
  • VarAC writes its own port numbers into VARA's settings when its settings are saved. VARA reads them only at its next start; "Check setup" shows the change and puts the two lines back.
  • Run at one station (WM8Q, Linux, since 2026-11-22) and on the bench. Not yet run on Windows.
